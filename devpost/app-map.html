Island Rush · Technical take-home guide

One run. A growing island.

A browser game where endless coastal runs earn the timber, stone, and coins used to build three island landmarks. One controller owns progress; Phaser draws the world; HTML supplies readable controls.

  1. Input and interface: src/ui/app.ts — search construction() and case 'build'.

    The interface displays the current recipe and calls c.build(). It does not independently spend materials.

  2. Validation, deadline, and persistence: src/controller.ts — build(), updateConstruction(); src/model/progress.ts — beginConstruction(), finishConstruction().

    The controller saves through src/model/save.ts. A pending record stores the original start and deadline; finishing does not charge again.

  3. Rendering: src/game/scenes/IslandScene.ts — paint(), paintConstructionMeter(); src/game/construction.ts — drawConstruction().

    The renderer reads the controller snapshot. It draws progress but cannot grant resources or unlock another level.

To change this, start here

Road width and lane alignment

src/game/projection.ts · routeLayout() and projectRoute(). Desktop width and the preserved mobile width are handled separately.

Road movement and surface

src/game/Boardwalk.ts · paintTrail() draws a continuous stone surface; paintEdges() moves subtle joins through the shared camera.

Island size and building sites

src/game/scenes/IslandScene.ts draws the township terrain, existing buildings, and small marked lots. src/game/islandMap.ts owns responsive map sizing and bounded pan/zoom. src/model/placement.ts owns clear-block anchors, occupancy, and legacy defaults; placeBuilding() validates confirmed moves without spending supplies. The world reads state; it does not spend supplies.

Endless pace and course streaming

src/model/runPace.ts integrates acceleration and exact contact times. src/model/run.ts streams deterministic encounters, prunes passed objects, and ends on collision or voluntary return.

Recipes, prices, and designs

src/model/catalog.ts owns recipes and offers. src/game/art.ts maps design IDs to original PNG artwork.

Touch, keyboard, and mobile screens

src/game/input.ts binds controls. src/ui/styles.css handles the bottom bar, separate Buildings screen, and HUD layout.

A practice to reuse

Check the geometry, then check how it feels in motion.

The first road revision aligned all three lanes mathematically, yet player feedback rejected its width on desktop and disruptive texture motion. The retained revision narrowed desktop only, kept mobile width, and separated a continuous deck from quiet moving plank joins.

The camera tests protect lane positions and screen bounds. They do not prove that movement is visually comfortable. The revision record connects the player observation to the implementation change.

On another game, decide what must stay visually stable, identify the smallest motion cue that communicates speed, and review real gameplay before expanding the content. Use tests for numerical rules and playtests for perceived clarity.
