# Island Rush — screen-recording guide

**Record here: https://island-rush-six.vercel.app**

Pair this with [the voice-over script](demo-voiceover.md). Record the real deployed
game, then cut the strongest clips to the narration. Target **2:40–2:50**; keep
the final export below three minutes. A live link does not replace the video.
Official rules: https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/rules (checked 24 October 2026).

## Before recording

1. Open the final production link in a separate browser profile or private window.
   This gives a clean island without resetting your usual save. Browser/origin
   saves are separate; keep using the production URL for the whole recording.
2. Use a landscape desktop recording, ideally 1920×1080 at 30 fps or better.
   Record just the game window. Hide notifications and unrelated tabs; leave
   browser zoom at 100%. Wait for all artwork to load.
3. Rehearse movement: Left/Right or A/D, Space/Up/W to jump, Pause, and Head home.
   The button labelled Head home has accessible name Return with supplies.
4. Record the voice-over separately first. Keep a clean version without music.
   Use short pauses and ordinary cuts to fit the actual footage to it.
5. Prepare a later-stage save through normal gameplay for the level-two, shop,
   relocation, and completion clips. Do not inject materials or fake a build.
   You may use a second recording profile for those clips. Clearly caption any
   jump forward, such as “Later in the same settlement.”
6. To prepare the first home reliably, two no-input runs collect 15 timber and
   2 coins each before the opening center-lane block. Each collision keeps the
   loot; 30 timber affords the home. Record one collision if needed, then cut
   the repetition. Do not use every preparation run in the finished video.
7. For a shop clip, earn at least 10 coins and select an unfinished timber-using
   project. Show 10 coins exchanging for 5 timber. At level 2, stone costs
   15 coins for 5 and is offered only when relevant to the selected recipe.
8. For the acceleration clip, record a longer run in advance. Keep changing
   lanes or jumping to survive. Capture the distance/pace display at two points.
   If cutting ahead, label the later section; never imply fast-forward is live
   speed. If needed, pause and use End run and return to bank safely.

## Capture order that saves effort

Record these as separate clips; assemble them in the story order above:

1. Fresh island, design selection, and placement before building.
2. Opening run with controls and floating collections; one collision and loot result.
3. Longer run with rising pace, then Head home and the result.
4. An affordable recipe and the complete five-second home build/reveal.
5. Level-two choices, shop exchange, completed-building relocation, and later
   completed settlement. Use genuine accumulated supplies and saved progress.
6. Phone/narrow-layout navigation plus reload. A responsive preview demonstrates
   layout, not a claim of testing a physical phone or swipe gestures.
7. Three planning documents and final live/repository links.

If you collide during a take, keep that footage: it demonstrates the retain-loot
rule. Use Run again or Return to island, then continue. Do not add a countdown
overlay—the game is endless. Do not call a grounded hazard a stone pickup.

## Editing and export

- Put the game on screen in the first second; keep title cards brief.
- Cut repeated collection/grinding, rather than speeding up unclear footage.
  Label time jumps, accelerated footage, and later-stage clips.
- Keep the build progress and final reveal at normal speed. Hold each important
  button/result long enough for a viewer to understand the change.
- Add accurate English captions if possible. Keep the narration louder than any
  optional audio and use only material you have rights to use.
- Export an MP4 with readable UI; check the entire file once with sound and once
  muted. Confirm it is below 3:00 and shows design → run → bank → build.
- Do not show terminals with tokens, account billing, personal notifications,
  the ignored learner profile, or survey answers.
